Pilates education inside a career-change market

The studio is not the market.

In Balance sells a way to become a pilates teacher. The people who might pay are also shopping a new working life, a government fitness qualification, and a way to get clients. The certificate is the product. The market around it is larger, and more crowded, than a pilates studio count.

AusPlay · cited 2025 1.63m

Australian adults who did pilates in 2025, up from 1.28 million in 2024. Participation, not purchases of a course.

IBISWorld · 2025–26 A$671m

Pilates and yoga studios. Revenue has been falling. The number of businesses has been rising.

Jobs and Skills Australia 35,900

Fitness instructors employed. Pilates teachers are not yet counted on their own.

In Balance · Jul–26 Oct 18

Online enrolments out of 126. About one in seven. The in-person business is the one being fed.

The decision this file is for

Where to point the next year.

The working constraint is not “is pilates growing?”. It is that In Balance can describe demand, and cannot yet describe a settled customer. Recorded value, media spend and enrolments are usable. Cash, margin, new-versus-repeat, and a true cost to acquire a buyer are not.

Lane 1 · Demand

More people are doing pilates. Studios are splitting a smaller pie.

AusPlay participation is up. IBISWorld’s pilates and yoga studio revenue is down about 3.9% a year over five years, to A$670.7 million, across 3,823 businesses. Teachers are entering a crowded room.

Lane 2 · A new working life

The adjacent education market is counted. The pilates slice is not.

Certificate IV in Fitness had 11,765 enrolments in 2025, on a compiler of NCVER data. That is the nearest counted “I am changing into fitness” behaviour. Nobody publishes how many of those people also buy a pilates credential.

Lane 3 · In Balance

About A$291,000 across 126 enrolments, and the online path is still small.

July to 26 October 2026. Media about A$66,000. A revenue-value return of about 4.4 times media. Fourteen percent of enrolments were online. Contribution after delivery is unknown.

Do not treat A$291,000 as settled cash, or 4.4× as profit. Ninety-nine of 126 enrolment records had a displayed amount. Currencies were converted on one 15 October rate. Deposits and plan amounts are mixed in. Partner delivery cost is only partly visible: two locked commissions, A$7,000 combined, not a full cost line.

Where In Balance sits

Upper middle of a split market.

Left is easy to start and thin. Right is a long government diploma. Bottom is a certificate. Top is a promise that you will also get work. In Balance sits in the middle: a serious mat and reformer qualification, in a room or online, without being the registered diploma.

The base hypotheses

Four reasons someone pays.

01 · Career change

A life event, then a different week.

End of a degree, a corporate exit, children at a new age, a death. The interview named this as the usual buyer. It is a hypothesis until the intent field and sales notes are counted.

02 · Already curious

They love the class and want to be the teacher.

The public page speaks to people who want flexibility around work, family and travel, and to people who do not live near a room. Curiosity is the warmest version of this, and the easiest to waste on window shoppers.

03 · Already in movement

A trainer or yoga teacher adding a tool.

The online page names fitness, health and movement professionals. They already know how to find a client. They are comparing a credential, not inventing a career.

04 · A business, not a PDF

The certificate is the ticket. Clients are the product.

Both the discovery call and the old Breathe pitch put “get paid work” at the front. In Balance teaches principles and assessment. It does not yet publish a client-getting system as the offer.
