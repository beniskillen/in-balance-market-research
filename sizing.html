Five layers. None of them is “the TAM” on its own.

Size the pool you mean.

A pilates equipment forecast, a studio’s class revenue, a gym membership, and a teacher-training fee are four markets. Published reports mix them, then disagree by an order of magnitude. This page keeps the layers apart and shows the conflict instead of picking a flattering number.

Two published “pilates markets” do not survive contact with each other. Grand View Research puts a global pilates complementary-medicine segment at US$2.12 billion in 2025 and a 26.3% growth rate through 2033. Consumer Industry Insights puts a broader pilates market at US$5.8 billion in 2025, and Australia at US$0.21 billion. IMARC puts Australian pilates and yoga studios at US$5.5 billion in 2025. IBISWorld’s studio industry is A$0.67 billion. The IMARC figure is about eight times the IBISWorld figure. This file uses IBISWorld for studio revenue and treats the US$5.5 billion line as unusable.

TAM, SAM, SOM

Three different sums. Only one is In Balance.

TAM · context

The ocean is participation and studio revenue.

1.63 million adults did pilates. Studios turned over A$670.7 million. Neither number is a course fee. The education market beside it, Certificate IV, is 11,765 enrolments. Use these as the world the buyer lives in.

SAM · who could buy this

A pilates teaching credential, in the places In Balance can actually reach.

Australia, New Zealand, the partner rooms, and online where an ad has run. The two sketches below are Australia-only serviceable cases. They are not the whole SAM, and they are not a forecast.

SOM · what is happening

126 enrolments and about A$291,000 in four months.

That is the only figure the business can operate. Annualising July to September is about A$0.85 million of recorded value. It is a pace, not a plan.

Serviceable, as scenarios

Two Australian sketches land near A$4 million.

That rhyme is a coincidence, not a confirmation. Both sketches are Australia-only, both use a A$3,500 blended fee, and both will move as soon as an assumption moves. In Balance also sells outside Australia, so neither sketch is a ceiling.

Studio seats

About 1,150 seats. About A$4.0 million.

3,823 businesses × 0.3 new teachers × A$3,500. If the hiring rate is 0.1, the sketch falls to about A$1.3 million. If it is 0.6, about A$8.0 million. The 0.3 is a placeholder.

Beside Certificate IV

About 1,180 people. About A$4.1 million.

Ten percent of 11,765 Certificate IV enrolments × A$3,500. The ten percent is not observed. It is a way to see that a small slice of the fitness-career pool is already the size of the studio-seat sketch.

In Balance pace

About A$0.85 million, if the quarter holds.

July to September recorded value is about A$220,000. Times four is about A$881,000. October is partial and strong, and the revenue is not settled cash. Do not put this in a plan as a forecast.

A top-down “share of the A$671 million studio industry” would be the wrong sum. In Balance sells education to teachers, not classes to clients. Studio revenue is the job market those teachers walk into.

What the labour market pays

The job on the other side is part-time.

A full-time fitness instructor’s median week is A$1,500 before tax. Most of the occupation is not full-time. Sixty-eight percent work part-time. A A$3,300 to A$5,999 course has to be sold against that week, not against a fantasy salary. The buyer who needs the course to replace a corporate income is taking a real pay cut unless they build a book of clients. That is why “get clients” is a different product from “get qualified”.

Pilates itself is moving up the industry’s own list. In the 2025 AUSactive-distributed survey of 632 people, pilates ranked third, up from eighth. For 2025–26, AUSactive lists it second, behind programs for older adults. The same survey was 71% female, average age 48, and 60% held a Certificate IV. Sixteen percent worked in boutique, pilates or yoga. Demand for the class is not the same thing as demand for a new teacher, but the class is no longer a side room.

ABS has split the occupation. It has not published the count. OSCA 462231 is Pilates Instructor. Gym pilates instructors stay inside Fitness Instructor (General). AUSactive’s annual report says it is working with the ABS so a future census can profile yoga and pilates separately. Until that count exists, 35,900 is the ceiling of the employed profession, not the size of the pilates faculty.
